Madan Mohan Malaviya

Madan Mohan Malaviya (25 December 1861 – 12 November 1946) was one of the foremost educationists, lawyers, and nationalist leaders of colonial India. A key figure in the Indian National Congress, he served as its President on four occasions — in 1909, 1918, 1932, and 1933. He founded the Banaras Hindu University (BHU) in 1916, which remains one of India’s most prominent central universities, and was also the driving force behind the Hindi literary movement and the founding of the newspaper Hindustan.

He was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna — India’s highest civilian honour — in 2015. The title Mahamana, meaning the great-souled one, is inseparable from his name and was conferred upon him by Mahatma Gandhi. To mark the 150th anniversary of his birth, the Government of India released a ₹5 commemorative coin in nickel brass in 2011. The coin was issued in circulation through 2013, and was struck at all four of India’s active government mints.

Coin Specifications

All five mint mark varieties of this coin share identical declared specifications. The obverse carries the Ashoka Lion Capital emblem with the denomination ₹5, flanked by “भारत” and “INDIA”, and the national motto “सत्यमेव जयते” beneath.

India’s Four Mints and Their Marks

Modern Indian coins struck for circulation carry a small symbol directly below the year of issue. This mint mark identifies the facility where the coin was produced. India operates four active government mints: at Kolkata (the oldest, founded 1757), Mumbai (Bombay, 1829), Hyderabad (1803), and Noida (1988).

Each mint uses a distinctive symbol. Mumbai uses a solid diamond (◆), Hyderabad a five-pointed star (★), Noida a filled circle (⬤), and Kolkata — as the oldest and original mint — uses no symbol at all, leaving the space below the year blank.

Beyond these four standard circulation marks, Mumbai has historically applied a script letter “M” to proof and uncirculated collector coins when they are struck on specially polished or proof planchets at the Mumbai Mint. Under normal practice, this “M” mark is never seen on coins intended for general circulation.

The Anomaly: Five Varieties from One Issue

The ₹5 Madan Mohan Malaviya commemorative is one of the rare post-independence Indian coins for which five distinct mint mark varieties are known. The four standard circulation marks account for four of these: no mark (Kolkata), star (Hyderabad), dot (Noida), and diamond (Mumbai). The fifth is the “M” letter mark from Mumbai — an exception to the usual convention.

According to the numismatic record, the “M” mark variety of this coin was released for general circulation, contrary to the normal practice under which the “M” mark is restricted to proof and presentation sets. This makes it one of a small number of Indian commemoratives where a letter mint mark appears on a coin accessible outside of collector packaging.

The “M” Mark: A Documented Exception

The letter “M” as a Mumbai mint mark has a specific and narrow conventional use in Indian numismatics: it appears on proof coins and uncirculated collector sets struck at the Mumbai Mint, distinguishing them from standard business-strike pieces bearing the diamond mark. The rationale is that proof coins, having been struck to a higher standard on specially prepared planchets, merit a separate identification mark.

For the 2011 Malaviya ₹5 issue, both the diamond (◆) and “M” variants from Mumbai were released as circulation coins. This is an explicit departure from convention and is noted in the numismatic catalogue record. The result is that the “M” mark variety, while not a proof coin, carries a mark that most collectors associate exclusively with proof issues — creating a genuine point of confusion and, consequently, collector interest.

The “M” variant is scarcer than the diamond variant, likely reflecting a smaller production run or more limited distribution into general circulation. Uncirculated examples in collector quality command a modest premium relative to the diamond mark variety.

Collector Notes

The 2011 Malaviya ₹5 is straightforwardly attractive as a set to assemble: five varieties, all from a single year, of a coin that circulated in normal commerce. The diamond and Kolkata varieties are easily found in uncirculated condition from dealer stocks. The star, dot, and “M” varieties require somewhat more searching but are not genuinely rare pieces in the broader context of Indian commemorative coinage.

The numismatic interest lies primarily in the “M” mark exception. A collector who holds the five-variety set has a tangible illustration of the way Indian mint mark conventions can depart from the expected pattern — and a conversation piece that links directly to the documented record on Numista (N 44250) and Krause (KM 405).

This coin was released in 2011 and issued in circulation through 2013. All five varieties carry the year 2011 on the reverse, regardless of the year in which a particular example entered circulation.
